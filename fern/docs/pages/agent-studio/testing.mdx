title: Testing
subtitle: Call your agent from the browser or your phone and watch each turn as it happens.

Every agent has a **Test** panel in the builder. Open it with the **Test** button in the header (⌘.). A test call goes
through your jambonz account like a real call, so what you hear is what callers hear.

There are three ways to test:

| | Who answers | What it costs | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **This browser** | the draft, or a published version you pick | no carrier minutes | Calls, marked **Test** |
| **My phone**, outbound agent | the draft, or a published version you pick | a real call through your carrier | Calls, marked **Test** |
| **My phone**, inbound agent | the published version | a real call through your carrier | Calls and Usage, as a normal call |

## Before you call

The panel shows what the call will use. Which options appear depends on the agent's direction.

- **Call from** (inbound agents) or **Ring me on** (outbound agents): **This browser** or **My phone**.
- **Version to call**: **Draft (unsaved changes included)**, or any published version. Unsaved edits are saved before
  the call starts, so the call always uses what you see in the builder.
- **Per-call values** (outbound agents): a value for each `env.*` variable the prompt expects. In production your
  system sends these with each call in `env_vars`.
- **SIP headers**: add `X-` headers to the test call as if a carrier or upstream application had sent them, so the
  agent's `sip.*` variables resolve. Each row shows the variable name the header becomes.
- **Record and keep the transcript**: on by default. Turn it off and the call is still listed in Calls, but its
  transcript and turns are not kept.
- **Audio settings**: the microphone and speaker for a browser call. You can switch either during the call.

The start button names what will happen: **Start test call**, **Have the agent call this browser**, **Have the agent
call** followed by your number, or **Watch for my call from** followed by your number.

### When the call can't start

The start button stays disabled, with the reason under it, when:

- jambonz is not connected: **Connect jambonz to make test calls.**
- the agent's speech or LLM credentials are missing: **Add speech and LLM credentials to test.**
- the draft has errors: **Fix N errors to test**, followed by the first one.

If a call fails after you start it, the panel says why in one line: for example a microphone blocked by the browser,
a registration jambonz rejected, too many test calls running for the account, or a carrier refusing the call with its
SIP status and reason.

## Test from your browser

The call runs over WebRTC from this browser. Your browser asks for the microphone the first time.

- **Inbound agent**: the panel calls the agent.
- **Outbound agent**: the agent calls you. The panel rings; click **Answer** to pick up.

During the call you can **Mute** and **End call**.

<Note>
An inbound test from the browser reaches jambonz from a SIP user, not a phone number. If the agent transfers to a
phone number and keeps the caller's number as the caller ID, carriers such as Twilio refuse it. To test that kind of
transfer, have the transfer show the agent's number; the panel warns you when this applies.
</Note>

## Test from your phone

### Outbound agents: the agent calls your phone

Choose **My phone** under **Ring me on** and enter your number (or an extension, for a trunk to a PBX). The call goes
out through a carrier on your jambonz account that has an outbound gateway; if you have more than one, pick it in the
panel. It needs a caller ID: set **Caller ID to present** in the agent's **Placing calls** section first.

These calls are real calls, and the account's **Outbound calls** settings apply to them: allowed country codes, the
always-blocked premium prefixes, **Calls per day**, **Calls at the same time** and, for Members, **Members can place
calls**. Emergency and service numbers are never dialed.

### Inbound agents: watch a real call

Choose **My phone** under **Call from**, enter the number you will call from, and click **Watch for my call from**
that number. Then call one of the agent's numbers from that phone. When the agent answers, the call appears in the
panel.

- The agent must be published and have at least one phone number routed to it.
- The published version answers, as it would for any caller. Draft changes are not on this call.
- It is a real call: it counts in Calls and Usage like any other.
- The panel waits 10 minutes for your call. One watched call per start; to watch another, start again.
- **Stop watching** closes the panel's view only. It never hangs up your call.

The call is matched by the number it comes from, so call from the number you entered.

## The live turn view

While the call runs, the panel shows each event as it happens, with the time since the call started:

- what the caller said, and what the agent said
- each tool call, with its name, whether it succeeded and how long it took (for example `lookup_order · ok · 412 ms`)
- **caller interrupted** when the caller talks over the agent
- **No response from caller · reprompted** when the caller is silent
- answering machine detection results, on outbound calls
- each step of a transfer or secure detour, including why a transfer did not connect
- call status changes and errors, with their codes

If you edit the draft during a call, the panel says **Draft changed. Hang up and call again to test the new version.**
The call in progress keeps the version it started with.

Test calls end after 10 minutes, and the panel warns you in the last minute. A watched call from your phone is a real
call, so this limit does not apply to it.

## Call quality

On a browser call, a strip at the top of the panel measures the audio arriving from jambonz:

- a verdict: **Good**, **Fair**, **Poor** or **No audio**, judged on the last minute
- the share of audio lost
- one bar per second for the last 60 seconds: green is clean, amber is under 5% lost, red is more, grey is no audio

Click the strip for details:

| Row | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Path** | this browser's address and the media address on the jambonz side |
| **Codec** | the audio codec and its sample rate |
| **Loss** | total loss, the worst 5 seconds, and seconds with no audio |
| **Jitter / RTT** | jitter and round-trip time, in milliseconds |
| **Network** | the network type, whether a VPN was detected, and loss in transit when jambonz reports it |
| **Mic** | the microphone in use, its peak level, and seconds it sent silence |

Loss is measured at the browser. When something is wrong the panel says so in one sentence, for example that your mic
is sending silence, that no audio has reached the browser for a few seconds, or what share of audio was lost in the
last 5 seconds.

## After the call

The panel says how the call ended and links to **Call details**, the call's page in
[Calls](/agent-studio/calls). There you have the transcript, each turn's timing and tool calls, and the recording if
the agent records calls.

### What the test report records

When a browser test call ends, the browser sends Studio a report of what it saw. It is stored with the test call and
holds:

- the outcome, the call id and the SIP Call-ID
- a timeline of each step, with milliseconds since the start: registering the test line, ringing, answering, and any
  failure with its cause and SIP status
- the media statistics shown under **Call quality**
- one row per connected second: packets received, lost and sent, jitter, bitrate, and the levels of your mic and the
  audio received

The same steps are printed to the browser console as they happen, as lines starting with `[test call]`, with the
test session and call ids.

On the call's page, test calls have a **Diagnostics** line built from this report, the recording and the jambonz
session. It checks each link of the audio round trip and gives one verdict: that every link checked out, or the first
one that failed. **Details** opens the evidence for each link:

| Link | Checked from |
|---|---|
| **Your mic** | the mic's level during the call |
| **Browser → jambonz** | packets the browser sent |
| **jambonz hears you** | transcribed caller turns, or your side of the recording |
| **Agent responds** | agent turns, or the agent's side of the recording |
| **jambonz → browser** | loss on the audio received |
| **You hear it** | the level of the audio received |

Under **Details**, a recorded call also shows the level of each side of the recording, one bar per second.
